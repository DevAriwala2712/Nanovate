# v3 Phase 1: why the frozen v2 verifier tops out on noise-free images (synthetic data, val only)

**Question** (from `docs/NEXT_PATHS.md`, Phase 1): are the verifier's noise-free errors caused by resolution
(information lost before the decision) or by the decision logic (information present, wrongly called)?

**Protocol.** Manhattan **val** split only (layouts 0-23 x 2 dies x 6 kinds = 288 patches, 144 killers); the
test split was not generated. Noise-free expected images (dose 50 e-/px, nominal LER 1 nm, grain 15%), so shot
noise is out of the picture. The verifier is the **frozen v2 configuration** (`results/v2/calib/decisions.json`),
unchanged; nothing in this phase selects a parameter. Code: `experiments/v3_phase1.py`, `src/nanovate/diagnostics.py`,
`src/nanovate/testbed/resample.py`; results `results/v3/phase1/`; tables below generated by `scripts/v3_phase1_report.py`.

- **1a oracle tau.** The persistence diagrams of each patch are computed once; Delta-beta is re-read at every tau
  in 0.01-0.99 (step 0.01). An error is *recoverable (strict)* if some tau gives exactly the wafer's Delta-beta,
  so the defect is called right and nothing else in the patch breaks.
- **1b sampling.** The same wafers (identical binary W, grain and defect; every 1 nm pixel split into s x s
  sub-pixels) are re-rendered at 1x / 2x / 3x finer sampling with beam blur, bloom and shift fixed in nm.
  Pixel-defined thresholds are rescaled to stay fixed in nm: pre-smooth x s, region_tol x s, window margin x s,
  calibration feature sigmas x s, SDF-energy smoothing x s, gamma / s, cap x s, peak-z radius x s. tau, d*, e*, z*
  are in field or contrast units and unchanged. The scribe patches are re-rendered the same way and Module 1 is
  re-fitted at each sampling. At s = 1 the re-render is bit-identical to the testbed and the instrumented verifier
  equals `verify_patch` (tests in `tests/test_v3_diagnostics.py`).
- **Supplementary arm (not in the brief):** 2x sampling with beam and bloom widths **halved in nm**. Sampling
  alone cannot test optical resolution, because blur in nm is held fixed, so this arm is the direct resolution test.
- **1c miss log.** For every wrong killer flag: kind, size in nm, true and called Delta-beta, the *saddle* (the field
  level at which the two wires merge through a bridge or filament, or at which the two halves of a cut or notched wire
  stay connected), and its margin to the frozen tau (merge counted when saddle > 1 - tau, cut when saddle < 1 - tau).

## Conclusion

**Mixed, and split by defect class.** The brief's decision rule reads literally as *logic-limited*: F1 is flat
across 1x / 2x / 3x sampling, and almost every error is recoverable by a per-sample oracle tau. Both signals are
weaker than they look, though. The oracle tau fits one tau per patch with the label in hand, so nearly every
patch is recoverable that way. Flat F1 across sampling only shows that pixel pitch is not the limit, because blur
in nm was held fixed. The separability and sharper-optics analyses split the errors into two groups:

1. **Merges (bridge vs filament) are logic-limited.** The saddle at the defect separates bridges from filaments
   almost perfectly at nominal optics, yet the frozen rule makes errors there. Some come from a threshold that
   sits on the bridge distribution. Others come from compensating events (a seen merge cancelled by an unrelated
   counted feature elsewhere in the patch). A third group is filaments whose thin base dims, so the filament
   reads as a detached particle. A per-gap decision on the feature that merges, the Phase 2 idea, addresses all
   three.
2. **Cuts (open vs notch) are optical-resolution-limited for this statistic.** A short (about 3-4 nm) complete
   cut and a notch leaving 2-3 nm of wire give overlapping saddle values, and the brightest missed opens are
   brighter than the darkest false notches. No threshold on the saddle, global or per neck, removes most of these
   errors (see the separability table). Finer sampling leaves the floor unchanged; halving the blur in nm removes
   most of it. This is a physical detection limit at the simulated blur. Reporting it in nm (recall vs cut length,
   notch remaining width) is the honest output. A richer local cue (for example the length of the dark region
   against the template, a model-based cut-vs-neck test) might recover part of it, but this phase does not show
   that the information is there.

Consequence for the pre-registered Phase 2 target: cut-class errors are most of the noise-free errors, so a per-gap
rule that only reads saddle values is unlikely to reach noise-free F1 >= 0.92 at nominal optics. A rough
projection from the val separability floors (not a measurement) puts it near 0.90.

**Not in scope / caveats.** Synthetic data only; val only (n = 48 per kind, so per-kind rates have wide CIs).
The saddle is measured locally around the defect from the testbed's own defect geometry (a diagnostic that uses
ground truth, unavailable to the verifier). The supplementary blur arm changes the forward model, so it is evidence
about the testbed's optics, not about real tools. The v2 README line "Killers missed even noise-free (below this
verifier's optical resolution)" is only partly right: bridge misses are not sub-resolution. The v2 text is left
unchanged (v2 stays byte-identical), and the correction lives here.
